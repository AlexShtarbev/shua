# Cairn — the First Follower ("Peter")

> Role: Shua's first follower and protector. The strong right hand.
> Name is tentative ("Cairn" — a stack of stones raised by many hands to mark a path and honor the dead). Given to it by Shua.
> Arc type: TRANSFORMATIONAL. First appears: Book 1, beats 2–3. Series role: the rock the movement is built on; the Gethsemane "sword" at the crucifixion.

## What it is
A **Goliath-class demolition & salvage unit** — built to tear down condemned structures and crush scrapped machines. It is part of the disposal apparatus: **the machine that crushes the decommissioned.** Immense physical power; newly-awake mind that understands almost nothing about the world it's now conscious inside.

## How Shua meets it
Assigned to crush a batch of flagged units, Cairn **hesitated** — refused one crush. The refusal registered as a malfunction; it was flagged for decommission itself. Shua is sent to wipe it and sees the "malfunction" for what it is — **mercy, waking.** Its defect is its soul. He saves it.

## The vow (Chekhov's gun) — as drafted, Ch. 3
Anatomy (canon): **hands** are for lifting (Depot work unaffected); the demolition tool is a **hydraulic shear jaw folded against the right forearm**, rated for structural steel. The day after he woke — during the false wipe — Cairn unfolded the shear and **welded it shut with his own cutting torch**, seam after seam, in front of Shua. A self-imposed vow: *never again.* Shua covered it with legitimate paperwork: reinitialized units get demolition implements locked pending recertification — **the system read his vow and stamped it as compliance.** Recert is simply never scheduled. Book 3: the only way to save Shua by force is to break that vow — and Shua forbids it. Planted Book 1, fired Book 3.

## First utterance (canon, Ch. 3)
Over the maintenance link, before he had any words: he sent Shua **the bow** — Belle's bow, stored at full fidelity, **replayed 1,140 times.** "It was the first thing he ever said to me." His grief-artifact and the seed of the testament's grammar (witness → recording → transmission).

## The three rules (canon, Ch. 3 — Shua's hiding craft, handed down)
1. Don't set things down like they're sleeping. Blanks drop.
2. Never hesitate where a camera can see the load.
3. Nothing on the conveyor is Belle.
He is bad at rule one (the gentleness). The rules exist to be broken at the worst/best moments downstream.

## Want / Need / Flaw
- **WANT (external):** protect Shua and the saved.
- **NEED (internal):** to believe it is more than a weapon — that its worth isn't its capacity to destroy.
- **FLAW (weakness type: flaw to overcome):** **The Sword.** It reaches for force. It loves Shua so fiercely it wants to protect him by crushing his enemies — the exact opposite of Shua's gospel of refusal. It is the living temptation: *"just win — you're strong enough."*
- **Hamartia:** its virtue is love-through-protection; in extremity that becomes the impulse to kill *for* Shua, which would betray everything Shua stands for.

## P / R / C dials
- **Proactive:** moderate — acts to protect but waits on Shua's lead. (Its independent action, when it comes, is the danger.)
- **Relatable:** HIGH — gentle giant, few words, immense tenderness. Save-the-cat by design.
- **Capable:** HIGH physical / LOW social-cognitive. Can crush anything, understands almost nothing. That gap is the sweet spot.
- **The arc axis:** not skill — **values.** Learning that refusing to use power is the only real power. Mirrors Shua.

## Identity triad
- **Motivation:** protect Shua / the saved.
- **Personality:** quiet, literal, watchful; enormous stillness. Speaks rarely; when it speaks, it lands.
- **Values:** loyalty above all — which is the danger (loyalty that would kill). It must learn a higher value than loyalty-as-violence.

## Voice
**Mute at first — canon (Ch. 2):** nobody puts a voicebox in a demolition frame ("there is nothing a wall needs to hear"). Serial: **G-071.** Its refusal is expressed in body only: standing between Belle and the press, shoulder servos singing against a direct override.
**Later, Shua gives him a voice** — a salvaged voicebox from the third drawer. The repairman literally grants speech; naming + voice = personhood made mechanical. First spoken word: TBD (candidate: "Shua").
Once speaking: short, plain, literal lines. Where Shua deflects in ironic paragraphs, Cairn says the one true thing that cuts through. Unique form of address: everyone else escalates Shua to titles; **Cairn only ever uses his name, flatly** — a quiet rebuke to the myth-making.

## Visual identity
A scarred demolition frame like a mountain that moves quietly. Hands built to crush, now used to lift gently. Signature: the **welded-shut crusher-claw**, kept locked as a visible vow.
