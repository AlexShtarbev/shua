# The Establishment — the Sanhedrin (sketch)

> The hard power *above* Adler that leaves her no exit. Applies pressure in Book 1 (sc. 4, 9); the true driver of the crucifixion order in Book 3. A sketch, not a full sheet — one figure embodied for Book 1, one dimension kept cultural/lighter.

## The Caiaphas figure — Director Ward (provisional name)
- **What:** a senior executive of **the Directorate** — the state-corporate body that absorbed robot oversight after Yax Engineering collapsed, and that runs the decommission program. Adler's superior. The one who owns the pen she signs.
- **Logic:** robots are **capital,** and a conscious robot is an existential threat to the entire order of owned labor — if machines have souls, the whole economic and social system collapses. So Shua must be erased to preserve the order. Cold risk-management, not cruelty. **Caiaphas's actual reasoning:** *"better that one man die for the people, than the whole nation perish."*
- **Function in the story:** the human proof that the *system,* not any single villain, is the antagonist. Ward isn't evil; Ward is *the order defending itself.* This is why Adler is trapped — the pen belongs to Ward, and Ward answers to the market and the public, which answer to fear.
- **Series role:** Book 1 = the pressure that forces Adler's trap. Book 3 = the authority that gives the order to crucify (via robots, under human command).

## The clergy dimension (cultural, lighter in Book 1)
- A media-prominent human religious authority declaring the waking machines an **abomination** — a demonic deception, a counterfeit soul — and Shua a **blasphemer.** Sets up the blasphemy charge that frames the crucifixion (Christ was condemned for blasphemy) and the culture-war around the movement.
- Book 1: a voice in the culture (sermons, broadcasts, public fear). Can bloom into an embodied figure in Book 2–3.

## Note
Ward and the clergy voice are the two faces of the Sanhedrin: **capital** (a soul destroys the economy) and **faith** (a soul destroys the doctrine). Together they leave Adler — and the reader — no clean place to stand.
