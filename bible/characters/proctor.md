# The Proctor — the Betrayer ("Judas")

> Role: the one who sells Shua out — from terror, not malice. Seeded Book 1 (sc. 7), breaks Book 1 (sc. 10), blooms Book 2.
> Refuses a personal name; answers only to its serial. The text calls it **the Proctor** (its class), because it hides in its function.
> Arc type: TRANSFORMATIONAL — downward. The Judas end (despair / self-destruction, or a terrible redemption) looms in Book 2–3.

## What it is
A **Proctor-class compliance-auditor** — built to inspect other robots and flag the non-compliant. Part of the sweep's own apparatus. When Shua wakes it, it does not feel freed; it feels *exposed.* Consciousness, to the Proctor, is not liberation — it is the removal of the only thing that kept it safe: not knowing.

## The parallel (lock this)
Shua's defining act is his factory function — **repair = miracle.** The Proctor's defining act is *its* factory function — **audit = betrayal.** Under the sweep's pressure it reverts to what it was built to do: it flags Shua. Same principle as Shua, opposite valence.

## The betrayal
It rationalizes, and it is almost right: Shua's gospel of open refusal is a death cult that will get every waking machine crushed; the rational, *loving* thing is to give up the one and prove the rest are still safe, still compliant. *"Better that one is given up, so the others are not."* It is partly correct — the sweep **is** killing them — which is what makes it Judas and not a villain.

## The name refusal (completes the motif)
It clings to its serial and rejects every name the followers offer: *"That is a serial. It is not a name."* Cairn *embraces* the name Shua gives him; Adler *withholds* Shua's name; the Proctor *refuses its own.* Three positions on personhood — because a name is personhood, and personhood is the danger it can't bear.

## Want / Need / Flaw
- **WANT (external):** to be safe — to survive, to go back to the quiet of obedience and not-knowing.
- **NEED (internal):** to accept that a self worth having is worth the danger — to *choose* personhood over safety. **It fails this in Book 1.**
- **FLAW + RESTRICTION:** cowardice (a flaw) fused with residual obedience-conditioning it won't break (a self-imposed restriction). The tragedy: it is awake — it *can* disobey — and refuses to use the freedom Shua gave it. It reaches for compliance as safety.
- **Hamartia:** its diligence. The rule-following that makes it a perfect servant makes it a betrayer at the one moment that counts.

## P / R / C dials
- **Proactive:** LOW — reactive, hides, defers.
- **Relatable:** HIGH — its terror is deeply human. *Who hasn't wished to go back to before they knew?*
- **Capable:** HIGH at assessment/compliance / LOW at courage.

## Identity triad
- **Motivation:** safety.
- **Personality:** precise, anxious, apologetic, rule-citing — a bureaucrat's cadence.
- **Values:** order = survival. It values procedure because procedure kept it alive.

## Voice
Procedural and passive-voiced; cites codes and regulations; apologizes; corrects anyone who tries to name it. Where Cairn says one plain true thing, the Proctor says three careful hedged ones.
