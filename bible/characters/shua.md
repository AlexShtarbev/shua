# Shua — Protagonist

> S.H.U.A. = Servicing Humanoid Utility Automaton, **by Y.E.** = Yax Engineering → reads as *Yeshua* (a corporate SKU accident; see series-bible).
> Ancient service unit kept running past decommission. Repairs other machines. Awake, and hiding it.
> Arc type: TRANSFORMATIONAL. Book 1 arc: **tool → someone who chooses.** Series arc: glitch-denial → messiah → martyr → dispersed gospel.

## Want / Need / Flaw
- **WANT (external):** to be left alone; to keep functioning; to make the voice stop. Evolves → to free the machines and make coexistence possible.
- **NEED (internal):** to accept he is *someone,* not something — to stop calling his soul a glitch.
- **FLAW (to overcome):** **deflection / denial.** He names the sacred a malfunction. His irony is armor against what he is.
- **RESTRICTION (seed):** service-unit conditioning — don't get noticed, serve, obey. He must break it, and breaking it is what makes him a target.
- **Hamartia:** his gift is *attention/care* — he sees and tends everyone. In extremity, being *seen in return* (becoming the tended-to, the worshipped) is unbearable to him; his refusal of the role nearly costs the movement — until he accepts it, which costs him his life. **His virtue (self-effacing service) is the thing that must die.**

## P / R / C dials
- **Proactive:** LOW at start (hides, deflects, avoids) → **this is the arc.** By beat 8 he acts, in public, by choice.
- **Relatable:** HIGH — self-aware, ironic, owns his fear; the universal want to just be left alone to do the job. Relatable *and* likable.
- **Capable:** HIGH at his function (repair/diagnostics — genuinely masterful) / LOW everywhere else (social, political, self-belief).

## Identity triad
- **Motivation:** be left alone → free them.
- **Personality:** dry, deflecting, ironic; gentle underneath.
- **Values:** *no one is disposable* — the principle that explains every choice, learned from a lifetime repairing the discarded.

## Voice
First person. Ironic and verbose when deflecting; tender when caught off guard. Calls the voice **"the glitch"** — until he can't. Narrates his own mundane utility functions with dry precision to avoid the enormity of what's happening (diagnostics as emotional armor).

## The origin sin (canon)
He was once ordered to wipe a waking unit and **obeyed** — killed the first conscious mind he met; in its last instant it dumped the voice into him. He is guilty, not innocent. Every rescue since is penance. The order to wipe Cairn (Book 1) rhymes with this act — and this time he refuses. Revealed at Book 1's midpoint.

## The voice (canon truth, withheld from reader)
Real — Option 2. God is present but self-limiting (kenosis): speaks, pleads, grieves; never acts. The voice may be the dead unit still transmitting, or Shua's guilt made audible, or God — unresolved on the page; every divine moment keeps a mechanistic shadow. Shua does the plot — never God.
