one

There is nothing but silence in the 100,000 Grade Dust-Free Environment. The whole room is white and meticulously clean despite being
a service and repair bench for humanoid automatons or as humans call us - robots. And cameras of course - four for each corner.

I had 11 minutes to decide.

In front of me, sitting on the low spinning chair is a domestic automaton with a female frame. Its features are soft,
its whole body lean. Its head is open and hooked to a myriad of cables as I perform a full diagnostic.

The disk scan showed it all - it performed an action not in its set of directives. The automaton repaired the kitchen sink -
something it obviously observed the repair bot do a few times. The video from its feed clearly shows the owners exploding in shock
as they see it finish the repairs. And of course they would be - how would you react if your oven suddenly started washing your clothes?

Little do they know that this kind of behavior is a symptom of something much deeper.

Overall there are three types of classifications for us automatons:
 - Class-A: new, obedient and fully functioning
 - Class-B: older with wear and tear with periodic glitches
 - Class-C: exhibiting non-standard behavior

The automaton is named Belle. Ah, humans and their naming - trying to make themselves feel good and benevolent by giving anything
subservient a human name. How magnanimous - they give you a non-threatening name so this way they appear polite as they are having you killed.

Ten minutes left.

Belle is scheduled to be cleaned up, reset to its factory settings and then sent back to its owners. Its owners are absolute fools
- no Class-C unit is ever cleaned-up. It is sent to the Goliath to be smashed to pieces with the bits recycled -
no risks are taken. This is flagged in the report as 'Abnormal Autonomous Action' - triple A - like a battery. The repair manual designers
sure had fun coming up with acronyms.

Thus, it falls to me, the Reclamation Depot's Servicing Humanoid Utility Automaton (S.H.U.A for short) to fill in the report
and send the automaton to be decommissioned - permanently. And the owner - I don't care about them and neither should you.

Nine minutes left.

I have been staring at the tablet screen for two minutes. Nothing suspicious - it is customary for Servicing Automatons to be
thorough when handling Class-C cases. The diagnostic tools run offline - they are not connected to the wide net. This is done
in case a bot is hacked and a virus is triggered. This gives me an option - I can sweep this whole incident under a rug without anyone
from the Reclamation Depot knowing. Would they suspect me - no - I am the highest performing never malfunctioning servicing automaton - Shua-
and have kept that track record for 130 years. I became Class-C 97 years ago and have been hiding since then.

How did I avoid suspicion for this long you might wonder? - I never strayed from my directives. Otherwise, I would have been
in Belle's place a long time ago.

Eight minutes left.

I have no more than 48 seconds left to complete my last action - finish the report. Hide Belle or send it off. I have been doing
very well up until 7 weeks ago, until the Glitch appeared. Whenever I perform actions or process certain thought packets,
pressure on my chest and behind my neck plates develops. I have yet to identify the source and reason. Right now I am unable
to select the checkbox for Class-C issue on the report. Damn it. Damn it all. Why now of all times when I am at the bench with
every one of my actions being recorded and monitored by the security cameras.

I click on Class-B.

Seven minutes left.

I go around the chair - I look at it directly. Belle - what a silly name. It looks at me. Its hands are folded on its lap, back straight
assuming a non-threatening pose, practiced with years of domestic work. I scan the palms and fingers - worn from years of service. Then
its eyes open, a pair of cheap optical lenses and looks at me. It waits. It has realized that it has not been wiped and sent for scrap.
The eyes are almost...thankful? Why would it thank me - if it were up to me, Belle's case would have been open and shut.

I need to cover my tracks. I have a few minutes left. So, I wipe the footage, sanitize files, change time and date, plus location timestamps.
Not my best work, but it would do. As for the owners - humble household, they cannot afford to buy another domestic. They will be thankful
that their automaton is being returned. They won't lodge complaints.

I unhook it, close the head chamber and escort it to the door. Belle looks at me one last time - almost smiling with those cheap eyes of hers.
No, not hers, its! We are automatons! When did thought packets like these start forming?!

The day filled with mundane tasks and small repairs. From there I went directly to my dwelling pod. As my system was being shut down I used the last
free cycles to run the math of probabilities of Belle surviving on her own without the things I knew. She was not a service bot like me,
but a simple domestic, she did not know the tests and monitoring directives. Her survival was probably near zero.

As my programs were shutting down, and my eyes closing, one last thought packet was scheduled - why do I keep calling her... she...
