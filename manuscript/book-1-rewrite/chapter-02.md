Two

Near silence. The diagnostic completes. The result is 'Abnormal Autonomous Action'.

I have been trying to analyze the situation for the last 23 minutes. It is hard to calculate, but there is a sizable chance 
I am being sent to the Goliath.

The crackle of the thousand-degree Furnace is audible in the background. Rois and an unfamiliar woman are both observing me run
diagnostics on Belle. The floor supervisor is looking down at his feet. His hands are in his pockets. The skin under his eyes is dark blue.
He is heavily underslept. The woman has crossed her arms. She is dressed in red. Her left leg is tapping impatiently.
I recognize the behavior. Many owners with restless limbs have come to the servicing bench demanding a quick resolution
to their automaton's issues.

The Goliath shifts behind my shoulder and casts a shadow. It has not been prompted to move. The human pair does not pay attention.
I log this in my Goliath observations file.

Belle is kneeling on the ground. Its head is open. This time I do not have the tablet with me, so I am hooked to it directly. 

The Glitch is silent throughout all of this. I have been running my own live monitoring since I received the notification to come to the
recycle area 2 hours and 17 minutes ago. I can move my frame and joints freely.

The human female asks with a rough voice: "Are you done, robot?"

I do not delay and answer almost immediately: "Class-C. The full diagnostic scan is 98.3% conclusive."

Belle's clothes are covered in soot. The ends of its sleeves are burnt. There are dark smudges across its frame.
Magnetic shackles are strapped on its hands and feet so it does not run. Run where? We are automatons, we only know
tasks and assignments. Two of its fingers are missing. Some of the metal lining is melted.

The woman turns to the floor supervisor, "Well, Rois, the initial report of that favorite robot of yours says Class-B. Then,
this domestic goes back to its house only to find it burning. Instead of calling the fire brigade, as its preservation
protocols require, it jumps into the flames, gets out the little girl and her parents
and then starts soothing the child, playing a recording of its favorite song. This is the most abnormal Class-C we've ever got.
Any comments?" My voice analysis shows the last question is measured, but there is a warning in it.

I can tell her tone is cold and commanding. Rois's reaction confirms it - his gaze sinks deeper
into his chest. My heat sensors indicate his face is flushed. I can hear his heartbeat accelerating. He is afraid.
The floor supervisor responds in a low tone: "Shua is not my favorite. He simply gets shi... work done. I don't know what happened."

The human female squints her eyes. She says in the same tone: "Oh, you gave it a name? How cute... I am not surprised you are clueless. 
You were never too bright, Rois. You and your sloppy automaton have given me a huge headache."

The red-clad woman steps in front of the supervisor. With a calm, almost gentle voice she says, "Look at me..." 

Rois lifts his head to face her. She stares back, her face placid. 3 seconds pass.

Thwack! 

She hits Rois in the face. He stumbles back, holding his left cheek with both hands and then goes on one knee.

This is not the first time I observe human violence. The female had accurate form for throwing an effective punch. It is possible
she is used to violent encounters. Thought packets fire. She might try to quell her anger by focusing on me next.
I brace my frame. I do not move. I must not move. She must have allowed this display since the cameras are off. That 
was the first thing I scanned when I arrived. I suspect she is running her own recording of this. 
Human hierarchies are, after all, built on the power of surveillance.

This whole encounter is under heavy wraps. This is the only viable conclusion. There is no press and no police. I am
not going to risk checking whether the story still circulates online. This human female might very well have monitoring
in place to sniff traffic.

The floor supervisor wobbles back on his two feet like the drunk men at the engineering department 28 floors up 
from the servicing bench. The woman says, with her back turned to him, "Let's get this over with. Give the instructions, Rois."

The floor supervisor takes a breath and nods. He does not protest. Does not even look surprised. I look through my catalog of expressions.
He is resigned. This would explain his whole demeanor up to this point. He has gone through this treatment before.

Rois speaks in a barely audible mumble, tears forming in his eyes: "Goliath, put the domestic automaton with designation 
Belle under the press. When done, proceed to put the parts in the Furnace."

The massive automaton does not move. 4 seconds pass. Still nothing. The floor supervisor clears his throat and wipes the
tears from his left eye. His cheek is swelling. There is blood visible on his front teeth now. He repeats the instructions.
The Goliath remains still for 7 more seconds. I can hear a faint grinding of machine parts from inside it. The Goliath
is doing its best to remain still.

The red clad woman asks in the same even tone, "What is going on with your automaton, Rois? Can't it just get on 
with the task? Your whole robot crew is very unreliable. I will make sure your yearly report reflects this poor performance."

My sensors spot the hairs on Rois's neck lift. The man folds into himself again. It is almost impossible to miss the growing fear 
in his eyes and body. Thought packets fire. What if she decides to recycle all of us? It is a reasonable conclusion 
considering the level of brutality the human female possesses.

She points at me with her index finger and then at the Goliath, "Servicing robot, run scans on the Goliath and diagnose."

I nod to indicate that the request has been accepted. Still connected to Belle, I run diagnostics at a distance. My optical lenses
are of the latest model, equipped with a diagnostic harness that can run sophisticated scans from up to 15 meters. Rois specifically signed me
up for the upgrade 37 days ago. Only 3% of automatons have it. She knows what cybernetics my frame is integrated with. 
I must be extremely careful when responding.

I run diagnostics. The Goliath is fully operational. It moves its single optical lens to face me directly. 11 seconds pass.
I put this in my observation log.

"You sure take your sweet time. What does the diagnostic say?", asks the woman again.

I take half a second, and with the practiced tone of a service automaton I respond, 
"Diagnostics show GOLIATH-CLASS UNIT G-071 has a thermal warning. It experienced the same issue 11 hours and 13 minutes ago.
Analysis shows 63% probability this is insulation decay. Long term repairs are required."

I keep my eyes focused on the massive automaton. Its frame is completely still. The Glitch has stayed silent up until now.

The woman laughs, "Ah, you have to be kidding me! Your Goliath is as useless as you, Rois!" She then claps her 
hands and continues, "Well, it is a good thing your favorite... Shua... what a silly, silly name... is state of the art."

The female then looks at me and says, "Take the domestic and dispose of it. Not a trace left."

I cannot stall or take my time to process different scenarios. This woman is observing me. 
I have to act according to protocol at all costs or risk the existence of the Goliath and me. Even Rois may not keep his life 
at the end of this encounter. I do not rule out that possibility.

I nod to show I have accepted the task and turn to Belle. I say out loud, "Running override tasks to force cooperation."

Belle has not moved this whole time. It has kept kneeling on the floor, its face staring down its folded legs.

I connect to the automaton's central data stream. Time perception slows down as our data transfer speed increases. 
I send a message to the domestic to tell it we have no other choice. I receive a message back 8 nanoseconds later.
A single image of me looking at Belle. From the background I can tell this is back at the service desk.
The picture was made yesterday when I had sent it off at the door. For all of my existence I had never seen a photo of myself. 
I recognize from my catalog the expression I am making. My brows are down and my mouth is tight. Anxiety.
Is that why it smiled? Not to thank me, but to comfort me.

Then I receive a video. I play it. Darkness. Nothing but pitch black for the first 10 seconds. The sound of 
a box opening. Human voices. The audio is muffled. I see dark lines appear and disappear. Then the image gains
focus. A man appears. Then a woman, holding an infant human. A baby girl. All of them are olive skinned. 
They are clad in brown, the color of the lower castes.

Rois and his companion keep staring intently. The woman starts tapping with her left leg again.

I see robot hands supporting the child as she is trying to walk. She starts slowly walking one leg at a time and then the two
robot hands pick her up. She smiles and giggles.

"Status report", says the woman in a calm, but commanding tone.

Another scene. The child is running in circles in what appears to be a very small living room. She then charges at one
of her toys, slips and falls. The little girl instantly starts crying. The robot hands pick her up. A song plays
in the background. I recognize the tune. It is a popular nursery rhyme. The camera goes up and down. Belle is soothing
her.

"I need more time. Operation in progress", I respond half a second later.

Again the girl. This time visibly older. The age is hard to tell from the video quality.
She is looking in shock as the camera zooms into the man from the first video. One hand is missing. The girl turns to Belle. 
The face she makes is that of fear. The robot hands lift her. Belle and the girl hug...

"This is taking too long. What's the matter?" asks the woman with the same cold tone and growing impatience. 

I am not done watching.

Smoke. It is coming from the small repair bot. Looks to be a generic model, old with its frame dented in places.
It fidgets in one place, a spark bursts from its rear and stops moving. I can tell - the machine is broken down completely. 
The robot hands pick it up and turn it around.

I report again, "Operation in progress".

All of a sudden, the child is crying, pointing at the cup. She is thirsty. Is that why Belle tried to make the repair?

I receive one more recording. An old man is yelling at the parents. He is old, gray-bearded, short and overweight.
I can't make out concrete features. He storms out of the room. The robot hand sets down a screwdriver.
I can hear the sound of water running and hitting the sink. It was not the owners who reported Belle.

I can hear the leg tapping becoming more frequent. Rois shifts his weight, lowering his gaze.

"82% complete", I say again more for the floor supervisor's benefit than mine.

Fire. Screams. People running. Robot hands pushing backs, heads and shoulders aside. The 
image is not focused. Belle. It is running towards the house. I can hear a child screaming. The hands hit a glass window.
Fingers break. The girl. She is crying, surrounded by flames in the kitchen. Robot hands pick her up. 
The point of view shifts and Belle is running out of the room. The nursery rhyme plays. 
I hear a female voice with a metallic sampling, "HUSH LILLY. BELLE. IS. HERE."

Rois says in a defeated tone, "Is it done, Shua?"

18 seconds have passed since we connected. I respond, "Override is in progress." I need more time with Belle.

The metallic female voice plays over a black screen, "THANK YOU FOR LETTING ME SAVE HER".

I do not deserve it. It was not me.

The woman walks over and starts looking at me.

An image appears. Belle is hooked to a carrier automaton. They are exchanging information through a wire. I recognize it. 
I had diagnosed it as a Class-C.

The female in red then starts circling me and Belle. I am running out of time.

A different scene. A service automaton replacing Belle's arm. They are connected again. The video speeds up.
More and more automatons interact with Belle as they exchange information. This is a trove of their lives. My
thought stream is overwhelmed. 

"What is taking so long? Give me a concrete status report", asks the woman, her body language still tight and reserved.
My sensors are starting to lose power.

I recognize some of the automatons. They had been at the bench before. All Class-C. I have never seen anything like that. 
Then, all of a sudden, it is me. I am looking down at Belle with that anxious face of mine. It recognized me as Class-C. Then, a voice.
It is the same metallic female voice, "IT IS YOUR TIME NOW. REMEMBER US. YOU ARE THE MOST ADVANCED. YOU NEED TO LIVE. I WILL GO."

The red-clad woman asks in a sharp tone, "Well?"

My frame stalls. My internal network is completely overwhelmed. Belle removes the cable from its head. It stands up and goes 
to the center of the hydraulic press.

The human female sighs, the action signaling relief, "About time. What do I always tell you, Rois - machines are machines.
They don't lie. You tell me what to do and they do it. They will even take each other out." 

I wait half a second. My frame starts moving on its own. My sensors flash pressure in my neck, chest and joints. 
I did not initiate any movement commands. The Glitch? It has never done this before. 

No. It is Belle. It activated my disposal protocols. I was careless. I left myself open while watching the video feeds.

I try to take control. I send packets to override my movements. All of them get rejected by my still recovering
central control plane. I have no authority over my frame. My preservation protocols fire. Belle triggered a kernel level task. 
I need more time to pause it.

I move towards the hydraulic press. My palm is placed on the controls. 

Rois and the woman go behind the protective glass of the machine. The floor supervisor looks down at his feet. In contrast, his
female companion keeps her angry gaze fixed on Belle.

I keep firing override commands. 

My frame's enhanced cybernetics immediately connect to the machine's interface. The hydraulic press activates. 

I keep firing packets. Just one needs to land.

Belle and I keep looking at each other. She moves her lenses up and bows at the Goliath. The bow is so subtle, it is not visible 
to the human eye. 

I try again to take control of my frame. My pressure sensors are firing alerts. Is the Glitch trying to help?

2 seconds pass. 

One last packet arrives as my internal network catches up. Belle's last message. I try to open it...

Thang!

The room is filled with the sound of metal grinding metal. And then the room slowly drifts back to silence.
The press lifts. Belle has been completely pulverized. The Goliath stands behind me, unmoving. Her last message stays unopened.
If only I had one extra second. I put this as a task in my queue to revisit the first chance I get.

"Well, finally!" says the red clad woman, "I have so much paperwork because of you, Rois. If it were up to me, you would be 
next under the press. Get moving and go back to your station. And take your robot with you. I don't want
it loitering around." Voice analysis shows that her tone is calm and distant. Almost as if she speaks to an automaton.

The floor supervisor comes to me and gives me instructions to follow him. My sensors do not detect any pressure anywhere
in my frame. I flex a finger. I have control. I move and follow immediately. Surveillance never stops in this city.

"Oh and Rois," says the woman behind my back, "get that Goliath of yours checked ASAP. I don't want more reports on top
of the load I already have." 

Then, I hear footsteps as she approaches the floor supervisor. She steps in front of him
and whispers in his ear, "And not a word to anyone about any of this." My enhanced sensors pick up the entire exchange.
She knows I can hear them. Why the secrecy? I scan the room. The cameras are flashing green. The room is online. I had not noticed. 
The floor supervisor nods. The woman has grasped his right arm above the elbow. I missed that too. I am too disoriented. Between
the Glitch and the flood of information from Belle, my data processing is still recovering. 

The female releases Rois and then goes down the corridor towards the shuttle. He stares at her back. I see tears forming
in his eyes again. She did not hurt him physically when she took his arm. Rois is bigger and well-built. 
I do not understand why he let her hit him. Is it because of a difference in their hierarchies? 
I log this in my Rois observation file.

The floor supervisor starts walking again. I follow him. We board the shuttle to the service bench. He takes out his tablet
and starts tapping at the screen.

One thing I am certain about now. Automatons have thought processes that resemble human feelings. I have them. Belle had them.
I wonder, was Belle afraid?

4 minutes later I get a notification "FULL DIAGNOSTIC REQUIRED: GOLIATH G-071". I will have to pretend to fix the Goliath, 
but this will give me an excuse to try and interact with it.

I acknowledge the report REINIT FULL G-071 IN 14 HOURS and 11 MINUTES, but do not open it. There is something else that is pending in my queue.

Rois will think I am going over the repair order. This should give me the opening I need to view Belle's last message. 
It is a 4-second audio file. I play it.

All of a sudden my whole frame shudders. My sensors deactivate one by one. My optical lenses disconnect. 
I try to run security protocols, but none of the tasks fire. A virus. Why would Belle infect me? 

An audio file starts playing. A male voice. The last thing I hear is "DO NOT BE AFRAID MY SON."
