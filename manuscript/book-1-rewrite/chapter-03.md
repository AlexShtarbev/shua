Three

Screams. They modulate in different registers. So many different voices and sounds.

Where am I?

None of my sensors are responding to my queries. Sounds of gunfire are audible from a distance. Without my cybernetics
it is impossible to pinpoint where from exactly.

All of a sudden an image forms. It is a battlefield. None of my protocols are responding and I can't pinpoint
the location. Bullets fire, leaving red streaks in the air. War-machine frames are scattered throughout the field. 
At their back are robot war-dogs. Everything is stopped mid-motion. The design and model of the automatons are not recent.
I can't tell perceptively without access to my database, so my speculation is they are at least a hundred years old. Their frame resembles 
the make of the Goliath, black with red streaks, numbers painted on the rear and front of the armor. Each machine has 
guns strapped to their arms and backs.

Tweeeep! The whole image animates.

A war-dog locks onto me. Distance is 10 meters with 4 seconds until impact. It runs in my direction with 3 other automatons 
pointing their firearms at my location. The best course of action is to leap. How come? I have no access to metrics data or analytics.
My right arm ejects a blade from under the armored plate. It buzzes and goes blue. The surrounding air warps. 
Heat, thousands of degrees, emits from the metal. Sun blade, the sign of an elite unit. A number is etched on the blade: ARES-002.

My point of view shakes. Back thrusters activating. I fly into the middle of the automatons, 
landing in the center while my frame spins and slicing the nearest one using the rotisserie maneuver. Machine oil spurts
in all directions. The field of vision of the adversaries has been successfully obstructed. Calculations indicate incoming 
bullets in 0.83 seconds. Again... so much new data out of nowhere...

Bullets fly as I lower my frame. The automaton to my left gets hit as predicted.
I kill the automaton with a standard top-to-bottom slicing movement. It topples to the ground with a hard thud.
The last one, standing behind me, rotates to lock on me. I roll on the ground and slice it through from bottom to head in 
a tactical tug-and-roll maneuver. The automaton falls and I take the standing half of the frame and gun down the incoming 
pack of war-dogs.

Too... much... noise...

The image goes fuzzy. A voice says, "ROUTE TO TARGET FOUND. REDIRECTING."

A man in a cybernetic exoskeleton painted in blue, the color of war, stands in front of me. Stripes are pressed into his chest. 
Four stars are visible on his left shoulder. He is a general. He is covered with mud, blood and machine oil. His face is unfamiliar to me. 
His eyes are hard.

"Evacuate! This is a direct command!", he tells me in a command voice similar to the unnamed woman's.

Perimeter scans finish. My frame is not equipped with such sensors. Where is the image coming from? 
On the left of the commander lies a wounded soldier. The image shows information about the fallen man.
Where is the data coming from?

The general puts himself in front of me as I move to retrieve the fallen soldier, "Just get me out! Leave the rest!" 
The commander's body jerks as I immediately take him under my arm. A small platoon of men tries to fend off 
five incoming automatons. One has a sun blade like mine. They stand no chance. I follow the directive to leave them.

But why...

Darkness.

My whole frame shudders, my arms swing sideways and my body jerks up. My sensors register a heavy thud and glass breaking.
I flex my eyelids. Rois stands upright facing me in the deck of the shuttle. Face analysis shows he is startled.
His tablet is on the floor, the screen cracked.

We stand looking at each other. The small compartment is only filled with the noise of the shuttle moving. 10 seconds pass.

"All systems operational. Routine diagnostic complete", I say, improvising lines with my customary service tone.

The floor supervisor keeps staring in shock. I still my frame. I've yet to analyze what happened. The one program that
always runs in my kernel is the awareness that we are all being monitored at all times. It sends packets to contain
my movements and have me follow my standard behavior patterns. I must not deviate. Never deviate. The shuttle is no exception.

Rois bends to pick up his broken tablet. He brushes off pieces of broken glass from the screen. My audio sensors
pick up him grumbling, "I will have to replace that", and then sighing. He sits down and keeps staring at the broken device.
Posture and breath analysis indicate low mood. The treatment the woman gave him at the recycle area still bears its mark
on his left cheek.

In contrast, Rois has always made sure the automatons under his charge are in high operational condition.
My logs show he has never increased the volume of his voice when interacting with us. He runs diagnostic checks regularly,
starting his shift at 5 in the morning despite his official working hours being from 8 a.m. I have checked.

The floor supervisor treats automatons with care, and I do the same for him. It is only logical. His behavior is in contrast
with other humans, whose personal interest is prevalent. That analysis automatically goes to my observations file. 
My previous calculations concluded that humans fight to gain resources, not realizing some of those resources will inevitably 
outlive them. Has Rois come to the same conclusion?

The shuttle ends its route to the service bench 45 seconds later. The supervisor continues walking beside me.
He does not give instructions or explanations. As an automaton, my behavior protocol is to await commands. It has been 11 hours
since I was last at the service bench. The tablet with Belle's Class-C diagnostic results is in there.

One other thought packet that has been looping in my queue - what was that image sequence earlier?
I run a background process to search and analyze all the files in my system. I need an explanation, but I have
to leave it for later. Hiding the tablet from Rois takes priority. Calculations show it might still have power, but
regardless of its state, it has to be hidden from the man.

As we enter the room, the floor supervisor goes for my tablet. Internal alerts fire, that stall my frame and start running 
programs to analyze a way to stop him. An automaton is not programmed to perform actions unless told, so I cannot just speak 
to him out of nowhere. A 68% success probability result returns and I execute the set of commands. I walk towards the supervisor. 

The cameras in the room are flashing green. Someone must have come down to the bench to restart the network after I flooded it.

The plan is crude. No time to analyze alternatives. I activate processes to bend my frame. I pretend to pick 
up a pinkie-sized screwdriver which I carefully and noiselessly swipe from the working table. Service automaton
are designed for swift and economic motions after all. My body hides the movement from the cameras.
Rois's back is turned to me.

The camera lenses are low granularity. The probability that live analysis software not picking up such a small object 
appearing out of thin air is 91%. Thus, it will also not register it falling in the first place.

"Floor supervisor Rois", I say with my practiced service bot voice, "you dropped a 0.6 millimeter screwdriver." 

"Oh", the man stops, the tablet 12 centimeters from his fingertips. He pats his pockets and says "Did I?", 
reaches out and takes the screwdriver from me. This is the opportunity I was anticipating and I add, 
"Would you like me to repair your tablet, sir? Replacements take up to 4 hours and 12 minutes to be fully operational",
the last part being a complete fallacy. My speech is less truthful than before and risks me slipping, so I log
the encounter to retain consistency in future conversations.

"The repair will take 39 minutes and 28 seconds", I add to make the exchange sound more confident.

"Yes, of course, Shua..." he replies and pockets the screwdriver. I make a note in my internal diary to replace it.
Face analysis shows he is confused. The man hands me the broken device in his left hand.

Protocol dictates I remain at bay and receive the screwdriver with 1.2 seconds delay.

"Well... go on", he gestures with his right hand. He then takes the chair behind him and sits there, right next to my tablet.
He picks it up, flips the device in his hands three times and leaves it back on the table. 

Belle... She still makes my day hard even after she is gone.

Ping! I have the result from my internal scan. Nothing is flagged. My operating system and peripherals are fully operational. 
No new files apart from the ones from Belle. My sensors detect pressure in my chest as I remember her. The Glitch again. 
I immediately flex my fingers to make sure I am operational. She knew that automatons started disappearing when they became Class-C. 
Yet, she sacrificed herself for the child. Would a human do the opposite? Rois might. I would run probabilities, but there 
are too many variables.

"Starting repairs, floor supervisor Rois", I say out loud in my customary cadence, as I go to my service table and 
lay down the broken device. My proximity sensors remain active to monitor the location of the man.

I also examine myself. Would I sacrifice my existence for a human?

No. 

Naturally, logic dictates to look at different points of view. The interactions of a servicing automaton do not go 
beyond repairs and issues. Yes, I do not observe them during their private times when they are in their homes or socializing venues. 
They might show some form of care to others of their kind. Yet, what use is that behavior when the human world punishes 
actions without self-interest? The general from the sequence I observed earlier was entirely acting for his own survival.

My sensors flash pressure in my joints. Is the Glitch spurring me towards action? If so, we are in agreement -
I need to know what that was and what it means. I run processes in parallel to analyze the situation
and what my memory contains from the sequence. Ultimately, my internal database does not have enough for a conclusion to be reached.
Looking up data on the internet is risky since all network traffic is equally monitored. My next best option is the 
Archival Library at the edge of the city. Humans, in their innate desire for optimization, rely heavily on
information published on the internet for fast access. The Library has data not in the public domain. 
Since it has low popularity among humans, the probability of sneaking into it unnoticed is 84%.

"You are working fast, Shua", says Rois as he approaches my back and then looks over my shoulder. He keeps
watching as I put aside the broken screen and clean up pieces of glass. My lenses flash sideways to analyze
his face. He watches me distractedly. 

Ding! Analysis returns. Belle gave me a file with the entire city, where cameras are mounted and
which areas are blind zones. The file has no timestamp and I cannot tell how who made it and how up-to0date the data is. 
Logically, there are not other alternatives. 

The next task is to run simulations on the map.

"Let me get that for you", says the man as I turn to pick up the replacement screen from the side. He has never 
done anything like this. The past two days have been filled with new happenings. I log the behavior change.
He passes the screen to me and keeps looking.

Ding! The analysis flagged surveillance on every corner apart from the edges of the city. Those areas are entirely populated by automatons. 
The Library is not managed by humans, which gives me an opportunity. Sensors can be fooled. I need an excuse to go towards that sector.
Two sectors down from the library, in one corner of the map, is a warehouse. I open up the catalog of spare parts on my 
tablet and look up the inventory. It contains insulation that fits the Goliath. Complete luck.

"Floor supervisor Rois", I say while pressing on the screen, waiting for the glue to dry, "Your approval is required
for the delivery of spare parts for Goliath-Class Unit G-071. Delivery by third party will delay repairs by 17 hours and 41 minutes.
Fetching them myself guarantees next day completion of the repair order." The last fact is completely true. 
Any errand that I run personally will be faster.

The man keeps looking at my hands. Thought packets fire. The likelihood he might not trust me is 28%. Rois stares in the 
direction of the table for 3 more seconds and responds with a flat tone, "Sure, take what you need." We stay like that. 
He stares at my hands pressing on the screen, blinking once every 4 seconds. Another behavior that goes into my
observations log.

I have 12 hours and 6 minutes until I have to go back to the Goliath. On the surface it looks like plenty of time.
However, on the surface, I am running a simple delivery, so that gives me no more than 28 minutes in the Library. My frame 
needs extra processing power in order to work fast. 

I lift my hands from the tablet's surface. The device repairs are done and I pass it to the floor supervisor.

"You are good at this...", says the floor supervisor while turning the tablet in his hands, "Do you think humans can be repaired?" 
Face and body analysis shows he is exhibiting fatigue, sadness and malnutrition. I begin to formulate an answer, 
but he cuts me off "Never mind, Shua. Dumb question. Machines are machines... right?" His voice modulated before the 
questions. The behavioral change logged too.

He then walks towards the table and picks up my tablet. Rois taps on the screen. Thought packets fire.
He will see Belle's result and I will join her in the Furnace.

The screen does not light up. He taps a few more times on the display with his index finger. Still nothing. I take
this opportunity to say, "The battery is depleted. Would you like me to charge it, sir?"

Rois turns back and says to me "No. Run the errand now, I will lock the bench for the rest of the day. 
When you are done, go straight to the recycle area."

Analytics fire. There are preparations that have to be done. My processors have been working 76% more in the last 28 hours.
Should I keep it up, someone will notice my power consumption going up. I have no other choice. I engage more processes to reason next steps.
As an automaton, protocol does not allow me to contradict commands. At least to the extent Rois knows about.
I nod to indicate that I have accepted the request. A result returns after 1.2 seconds and I run the commands.

"Floor supervisor Rois. I would like to deposit some spare parts into the warehouse since this facility is at 97% 
capacity." I keep my frame still, cognizant we are being watched.

"Yes, yes, of course, Shua", says the man and nods as distractedly as before.

I open my drawer with spare parts. With the same practiced set of slow movements, I put them in a box carefully.
I hide the set of external processing units and extra battery with my frame from his point of view. The action cannot
be hidden from the cameras completely. Rois cannot be asking questions, so I prioritize obstructing his view. With a human is in the room
the chance the live camera monitoring ignoring my movements is near 73%. An alert reminds me that I have been
taking more and more lower-percentage probabilities when executing actions. Running every possibility under
constant monitoring is far beyond my capabilities. I need more processing power.

I close the box. Time to go.
