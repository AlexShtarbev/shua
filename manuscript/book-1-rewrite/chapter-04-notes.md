## THE METHOD — it is punctuation, not length

The thing being measured is not "short sentences." It is **whether the verdict gets its
own sentence.** The rule (§1.2): roughly a third of narration sentences run four words or
fewer, *and none of them wallpaper* — each marks a reaction, verdict, camera cut, or
turn. **The test is function, not frequency.** Supporting rules: §1B.2 (after a long
sentence, drop short — 28% of ≥12-word sentences are followed by a ≤5-word punch) and
§1.4 (the last sentence of a paragraph is the stressed position; land the quotable thing
there).

**The diagnosis, precisely: you are now JOINING with conjunctions what Ch. 1 SEPARATED
with full stops.** Same content, same word count, opposite rhythm.

> **Ch. 1 (28.5%, in band):** "No choice. I click on Class-B. The report is filed."
> "I try to execute the movement again. Nothing happens. I flex my fingers. Good. I can
> move. I try to click Class-C. I stall."
> **Ch. 4 escort (≤4-word rate: 0%):** "It did not even take a half a second to consider.
> It has good decision-making capacity **and** seems adept at hiding its Class-C
> reasoning." · "It can change its contents at any point, **so** trusting that is not
> enough."

In both Ch. 4 examples the punch is *already written*. It is just welded on with a comma
or an "and" instead of standing up by itself.

**THE MOVE, in three steps.** ① Find the seam — usually `, so` · `, but` · ` and ` ·
`whose` · `which` · `in case`. ② Ask what sits after it. If that tail is a VERDICT, a
DECISION, a CONSEQUENCE or a REACTION, it is a punch. ③ Replace the seam with a full stop
and cut the tail to its bones. Nothing else changes. **Cost: zero words. Often negative.**

**The paragraph test (do this one first, it is the highest yield).** Read only the LAST
sentence of each paragraph. In this chapter's live zone, every single one is 9–37 words —
not one paragraph lands on a punch. The stressed position is being spent on reasoning
instead of on the thing the reasoning produced.

**What NOT to do.** Do not chop everything; uniform shortness is a different, worse
problem than uniform medium. The target is CONTRAST — long, then short. And do not
manufacture beats: if the tail is not a verdict, leave the sentence alone.

**Your own corpus, for calibration** (all from Ch. 1, all ≤4 words, none of them choppy):
"Four cameras." · "Good." · "Nothing suspicious." · "Same bench." · "Nothing happens." ·
"I can move." · "I stall." · "No choice." · "The report is filed." · "It waits." ·
"It smiles." · "That can't be." · "This is a first." · "Diagnostics are conclusive." ·
"A cough." · "I do not turn." · "Silence." · "Thang!" · "He stops." · "A woman's voice."
· "A news report." · "The rain has stopped." · "The Glitch is silent."
That is what 28.5% looks like. It is not staccato. It is your best writing.
