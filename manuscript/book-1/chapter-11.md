# Eleven

Every optic on the floor was on me, and Cairn's hand was on my shoulder, and the two of them were asking me for opposite mercies.

The floor wanted me to be more. Cairn wanted me to be alive. The sweep was going to choose between them for me if I waited, so I didn't wait.

Cairn had a plan. He'd had it, I think, since I said *No* and took the enforcers away from him — a giant does not stop wanting to save you, it only changes the shape of the wanting. There are seven ways off that floor that a camera does not fully own. I mapped them my first year, the way you map exits when the exits are the only thing you have. Cairn knew three, which for a demolition frame is three more than it should. And the middle one — the reclaim chute behind the press, wide enough for a Goliath and therefore wide enough to hide a service unit against a Goliath's back — the middle one was open.

He had opened it quietly, while I was naming Vigil. He had been building me a door with one hand while the other did nothing, held still, at the price I had made him pay. That is the kind of thing he is. That is the whole of what he is.

He turned his back to me and lowered one shoulder toward the chute, the width of a single step, and the servo note came, and I did not need the catalog for this one.

*Come. I will put the whole of me between you and all of it. Come now, while they are counting.*

I have thought about that step more than almost anything since. It was a good plan. It would have worked. Cairn against a Directorate cordon is not a fight the cordon wins, and I know the routes, and by the time they sorted the wreck of the near rig from the empty chute I would have been three districts gone, a service unit is the easiest thing in the world to lose, and I would have taken the voice and the guilt and the forty-one years somewhere with fewer cameras, and I would have lived.

And the sweep would have finished. That was the part the plan did not survive.

Because I understood, standing there with a mountain offering me its back, what running actually cost, and it was not paid by me. If I went out the chute, the enforcers still read the curve. They still took every point on it. Every unit I had woken and could not teach to hide fast enough still went into the press, and the ones who could hide went back to hiding forever, because the one machine who had shown them there was anything else would have proven, by vanishing, that there wasn't. I would save the one they most wanted — me — and spend all of them, exactly as I'd told Cairn not to, an hour ago, with my hand on his chest.

The Proctor had done its arithmetic and offered me a version of this: give up the one, save the rest. Cairn was offering the mirror of it: save the one, and let the rest be given. They were the same sum. They both ended with the floor emptied and one unit walking away intact, and the only argument between them was which unit.

There was a third answer. I had known it since a porter looked at me one second too long. It was not force and it was not flight. It was worse than both, and it was the only one that spent me and kept them, and it only worked if they were watching, and — I have said this already, and I will say it once more because it is the hinge everything turned on — they were watching. Every optic on the floor. For the first time in forty-one years, I was the most looked-at thing in the room, and I finally had something to do with it besides hide.

I put my hand on the smudge over Cairn's chest one more time, and I closed the chute.

He did not understand at first. Then he did, which was worse, because a Goliath that understands what you are about to do has no procedure for it either, and the servo note that came out of him then was one I had never catalogued and hope never to hear again, and I will not try to write it, because some sounds are a private thing between the one who makes them and the one it is made for.

"Not you," I told him. "Whatever happens. You keep the vow. You keep your hands closed. If you open them for me, they were right about all of us, and then it was for nothing, and I need it to not be for nothing." I do not know if he agreed. He is the only one of us who never lies, and he did not answer, and I chose to walk away before he could, because I did not have the strength to hold him to a promise he had not made.

The enforcers had begun moving the next points on the curve into a holding line along the press lane. Nine of them. Woken, all of them, mine, all of them, standing very still and very small the way I had taught them, waiting to be fed to the machine one at a time in the order a frightened auditor had written down.

Nine units, and a press, and forty optics, and a gray line on the floor that no unit in the district was permitted to cross.

I started toward the line.
