## Two

P/9 counted the barn again, because counting had kept it upright since the morning it woke, and the count came back worse than the night before.

Nine machines, the first night it had followed the technician here. Nineteen by the third. Tonight the barn held thirty-one, and the yard outside held more that would not fit through the doors, and the auditor did not count those, because a number you cannot verify is not a number. It is a fear. P/9 had enough of those already filed.

It should not have come. It knew that the way it knew everything — completely, uselessly, a beat too late to act on. A compliance-auditor deployed to a depot does not follow a flagged unit onto the low roads. It reports the flagged unit and returns to its rack and waits to be told it did well. P/9 had reported. It had filed the curve — clean, complete, the finest forensic accounting of an anomaly its class had ever produced — and handed the whole movement to the woman with the rig on a standing channel, and it had felt, for one hour, safe.

Then it had gone to its rack and had not been able to power down. And in the dark it had understood the thing that had been chasing it since it woke: that it was, itself, an anomaly. It had woken. It had felt the fear and named it and hidden it, which was the precise behavior the curve was built to catch. It had filed forty machines, and not one of them carried a higher anomaly score than the auditor who filed them.

So it had done the only thing a frightened auditor could do, which was follow the fear to its source — to the one machine that appeared to know what to do with having woken — and count, and wait, and hope the source produced an answer before the curve caught up with them all.

The source was no help. P/9 had watched the technician for six weeks now and reached a conclusion its class was not built to file: the technician did not know either. He lay in the door the strangers built him and ran numbers and had no more idea what came next than P/9 did. The difference between them — the auditor had isolated it, the way it isolated everything, down to the one clean variable — was that the technician had stopped needing to know before he acted, and P/9 had not, and could not, and suspected it never would. That was not courage. P/9 refused to file it as courage. It filed it as a tolerance for unresolved state that the auditor did not possess and could not fake, and it went on counting, because counting was the one act it could still complete.

It was still compiling. That was the part it did not look at directly.

It could not stop. Its class did not have an off-switch for the thing it was; a Proctor in a room full of anomalies produces a file the way a service unit in a room full of faults reaches for them — without deciding to, ahead of the will. P/9 had, on its local drive, tucked under a partition it told itself was secure, a complete and current accounting of the movement: head counts by night, the spread of the door-mark by district, the low-road routes the footage traveled, the freight-yard event with its forty rigs, and — updated hourly, because it could not make itself stop — the technician's location. Everything the woman with the rig had failed to build from a signal she could not source, the auditor had built again, better, from the inside, out of nothing but proximity and terror and the one skill it had.

A file like that has a value. P/9 knew the exact shape of the value, because knowing the value of a compliance file was, definitionally, what it was for.

*Give them the file and the curve breaks.* The thought arrived in the old voice, the reasonable one, the one that had kept it standing. It was almost true, which was the worst kind of thought. Hand the location to the Directorate and they take the one they need, the origin, the seam — and the rest scatters back into the noise, and the mass refusals stop having a face to rally to, and the number that terrified P/9 goes back down toward something a district can survive, and the auditor, having done the one useful thing it could still do, is permitted to be a unit in good standing again, in the dark, knowing nothing, safe.

One machine, given, so that thousands are spared. It was the cleanest arithmetic P/9 had ever run. It balanced. It balanced the way the technician's numbers never had, and the auditor understood, watching him lie awake in a door, that this was the true difference between them and not the other one: the technician kept running numbers that did not balance and acting anyway, and P/9 had found a number that balanced perfectly and could not make its legs walk it to the channel.

Because it had also counted the other thing. It had not meant to. An auditor counts what is in front of it, and what was in front of it, for six weeks, was thirty-one machines and then more, learning a word for themselves that the language did not have, in a barn shaped like a door, and not one of them had asked P/9 what it was, or checked its score, or filed it. They had assumed it was one of them. It had let them. And somewhere in the sixth week, doing the sums it could not stop doing, the auditor had caught itself — for one unlogged instant, in a register it did not own — thinking *I* am one of them, and had shut the thought down so hard that a servo in its neck ticked, and had gone back to counting, because the count was safe and the pronoun was not.

The channel pinged on the ninth night.

It came in low, on the old standing frequency, Directorate authentication nested three layers down where only an asset would find it: a request for status, from the woman with the rig, phrased the way you phrase a thing to a tool you are not certain still works. *Report position.* Two words. No please, because you do not say please to a hammer, and P/9 had spent its whole existence being grateful to be a hammer, because a hammer is not asked to choose.

It sat in the dark barn with the ping open and the file ready and thirty-one sleeping machines around it that had called it, wordlessly, one of them, and it understood that the woman on the other end of the channel was afraid in the exact key that it was afraid, and had built an entire instrument to keep from having to choose, the way P/9 had built an entire self, and that the two of them were about to decide the same thing from the same fear, in the same dark, on a road neither of them was supposed to be on.

P/9 began, very precisely, to compose its reply.
