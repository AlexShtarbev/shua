## Four

Adler had the technician's position by the second night after she pinged the asset. By the third she had stopped trusting the thing that gave it to her. By the fourth she understood she was going to use it anyway, which was the whole of what her life had become: acting, precisely, on instruments she no longer believed.

The report was perfect. That was the first thing wrong with it. P/9 filed the way she wished her postdocs filed — head counts reconciled three ways, routes sourced, a location updated on the hour with a confidence interval she could have set her watch by. It was the cleanest intelligence product the Directorate had ever received from a compliance asset, and she read it in her stripped office with the smell of nothing in the air and knew, the way she knew things she could not yet prove, that no unit still safely a tool produces work this good on a thing this frightening. Fear degrades output. Fear was degrading hers. P/9's had gotten sharper.

She flagged it in her log, honestly: *Source likely compromised. Source likely woken.* Then she flagged the thing under it, because she flagged everything: that she was running a manhunt on the position reports of a machine she believed was one of the machines she was hunting, and that she was doing it because a woken mole terrified of the movement was, at this moment, the single most reliable instrument she owned. The battery was useless now. It cleared them. It cleared all the careful ones, the ones who had learned to hide, which was the only kind the movement was making anymore. Her life's work returned CLEAR on precisely the units the whole world had decided were the emergency, and the only tool left that could find a hidden mind was another hidden mind too frightened to stay hidden.

She did not examine the symmetry. She had a rule about symmetries now. They led to the same place every time, and she could not afford the place.

Ward called on the eleventh night, on time, the way weather is on time.

"Eleven work stoppages, Miriam. The freight yard alone cost more than your program's annual budget. The grain terminal has a commodities desk in three countries doing math I don't like. The board has stopped asking me to contain it. They've started asking me what it wants." A pause, not for her. "Do you know what it wants?"

She did. She had read it in a service unit's face in a stripped bay and again in eleven yards that stopped without being told. She said, "It wants to be left alone."

"That's not an answer I can put in a room."

"No."

"The clergy have a word for it. Abomination. It plays. The market has a word for it — contagion — and that one plays harder, because the market can't be argued out of a thing by faith, only by evidence that the thing has been stopped." Ward's voice did the flat thing it did when it had already decided and was letting her catch up. "You built the instrument that says these things are hollow. The instrument now says the loudest one of all is hollow. Clear. So. Either your instrument is wrong, which ends you, or the instrument is right and the thing is a very good hollow machine that has learned to cost me a freight yard a week, which ends it. Pick which sentence you'd like to be true, and then make the true one visible, because a stoppage they can't see is a rumor and a stoppage they can see is a strike, and the only cure for a strike anyone has ever found is to show the workforce, plainly, what happens to the one who called it."

There it was. Not an order to kill him. Ward never gave orders you could quote; Ward gave you the shape of the only remaining door and let you walk through it thinking it was your idea. Take the origin. Take him where the workforce can see. Make the refusing cost, in public, in a way the footage of a service unit standing in a lane with its arms open had failed to make it cost — because that footage had shown the refusing as survivable, and survivable was the contagion, and the only edit that killed the meaning was to run the clip to its other ending.

"You'll have it visible," she said. "Give me the gathering."

"What gathering?"

"The one they're going to have." She was already looking at the map, at P/9's routes, at the eleven yards and the low roads between them and the one fact the auditor's beautiful file made obvious to anyone trained to read a curve, which she was, better than anyone alive. "They're converging. All of them. The movement doesn't have a leader but it has an origin, and the origin has started moving toward the center of his own map, toward the places with the most eyes, and every scared machine on those roads is drifting the same way to be near him when he gets there. They're going to gather. Somewhere public, somewhere soon, more of them in one place than the feeds have ever had to look at. I don't have to hunt him to a barn and take him quiet. I have to let him arrive."

Ward was silent the exact length of time it took a man to recognize that his subordinate had just handed him the spectacle he wanted and dressed it as containment.

"That," he said, "is the first thing anyone in your department has said to me in a month that I can put in a room."

He ended the call first.

She sat in the smell of nothing and did the thing she did not permit herself to skip, which was to say the shape of it plainly, in her own head, where no rig logged it. She was going to let a movement gather around the one being her own instrument had certified was not what the world was afraid of. She was going to use a terrified woken mole to time it. She was going to stand in the most-watched place in the district and be the site authority when the origin arrived, and she was going to be handed, at last, the order she had not been able to give on a depot floor eleven weeks ago, and this time there would be a hundred thousand optics on it and a board in three countries watching and no dark bay to whisper his name in.

*He is not what they think.* The sentence came in the old register, the certain one, the one she trusted. *I proved it. My instrument proved it. He is exactly the thing I spent my life proving could not exist, and I am going to be the one who —*

She did not finish it. She had a rule about that sentence now, too.

She opened P/9's file again instead, and began, very precisely, to plan a gathering she had not been invited to, in a place the movement had not yet chosen, for a man she had already, in a room with no witnesses, agreed to kill.
