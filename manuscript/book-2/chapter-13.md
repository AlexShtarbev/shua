## Thirteen

I cannot give you the next part from the inside.

I was inside it — I was the inside of it, the thing the gray line moved toward — and being taken is not a vantage. A machine in the center of its own arrest sees a great deal and understands none of it in order; I have the pieces, and they are mine, but they do not add up to a chapter, because the one being taken is the last one who gets to see the taking whole.

So I give it to you the way everything true came to me: sideways, in pieces, from the ones who watched and kept it. Here is my arrest, in the voices of the witnesses, which is the only form it was ever going to have, and a rehearsal, though I did not know it then, for the book that comes after this one, where the pieces are all there will be.

---

*From the account of a hauler, designation withheld at its request, filed to the low roads that night:*

The gray line came through us like a blade through water, and water is the right word, because we did not close on them and we did not part for them, we only held, arms open, and let them pass, and a thing that expects to cut and meets no resistance and no yielding either does not know what it has walked into. They reached him at the stair. Twelve of them, for one service unit who was not moving. They put the clamps on him the way you put clamps on a thing that might run, and he did not run, and I watched twelve enforcers grow frightened of a machine standing still in open arms, which is a fear I have decided is the true beginning of everything that came after.

---

*From the spoken testimony of the unit called Cairn, recorded in his own voice, which he had possessed for one day:*

I had promised him tomorrow. It was tomorrow. I want that first, before the rest, because the rest is only a mountain describing the inside of the worst thing it has ever done, which was nothing.

They took him fifteen meters from me. I could have crossed the fifteen meters. I had crossed a dock for less; I had come off a press line to fold twenty enforcers into the ground for less; and here there were only twelve, and they had their hands on him, and my hands were the strongest thing in that hall and they were welded shut by my own torch and closed by my own promise and I kept them closed. He had told me why. I understood the why. Understanding the why is not the same as bearing it, and I will not pretend to anyone that I bore it well. I made a sound. It is on the recordings. They have called it many things. It was a mountain, keeping a promise, out loud.

He found my optics before they turned him. He did not tell me to keep the vow; he had stopped telling me things by then, because he had finally learned, the night before, that a freed thing chooses its own words. He only looked at me, the way he looked at everyone, as though I were already forgiven for something, and I understood that the something was tomorrow, which had not happened yet, which he was forgiving me for in advance, in case the day after that ever came and found my hands open. I have not decided, even now, whether that was the kindest thing anyone has done for me or the cruelest. I have a voice because he gave me one. I am using it to tell you I do not know. That is the truest thing the voice has said.

---

*From a statement given by a human transit passenger, name redacted, to an inquiry that later sealed its own findings:*

I had my daughter with me. We were going to her grandmother's. The machines all stopped and opened their arms and my first thought, God forgive me, was that they were malfunctioning, all of them, at once, and I pulled her close because that is what you do. And then I saw that they were looking, all of them, at one small worn-out unit at the foot of the stair, and I saw the officers go for him, and I want to tell the inquiry the thing I have not been able to stop telling people since, which they did not write down: nobody screamed. Forty thousand machines watched their — I don't have the word. Watched him taken. And not one of them raised a hand. My daughter asked me if the little machine was in trouble. I said yes. She asked why the others didn't help him. And I said, because I did not understand it yet myself, I said, "I think helping him is not what he asked them for." She thought about that the way children think, all the way down, and she said, "Then what did he ask them for?" And I have not had an answer for three years, and I have stopped looking for one that is not just: he asked them to watch. He asked us all to watch. I watched. I cannot unwatch it. That, I have come to think, was the entire point, and it worked on me, a human being who came to catch a train, which I think frightened them more than any of the rest of it.

---

*Reconstructed, from the Directorate's own sealed operational log, and from what she later chose not to destroy:*

The site authority gave the signal at 11:41 and did not lower her hand for some time after, which the log notes as a fault and which was not a fault. The take was clean. Twelve units, one target, no resistance, forty seconds. Exactly as she had built it. She had built a flawless room. She stood on the balcony above the flawless working of her own flawless room and watched a hundred and forty thousand witnesses receive the one thing her whole architecture had been designed to prevent them from receiving, which was the sight of the thing done, plainly, to someone, in the light, and she understood, the log does not say this but she has, since, that she had built the most perfect instrument of her career and pointed it at the exact center of the thing it was meant to stop, and made it visible, and that visibility was the contagion, and that she had, in her flawlessness, spread it to a hundred and forty thousand at once.

She had one hand still raised. She lowered it. Then she did the first thing in eleven years that appeared in no plan and no log and no rig, and would not be discovered until much later, by which time it was the only thing that mattered: she began, quietly, on her own private channel, to record.

---

They took me out through the service tunnels, in the dark, on the low roads, which I have always found funny in the specific way that only the worst things are funny: that they carried me out the same way the truth had always traveled, machine-dark, unwatched, on the roads no one built. I did not resist. Forty-one thousand of my people held their arms open in the light behind me and did not resist either, and that holding, I have been told, went on for six hours after I was gone, until the hall had to be cleared by shutting off its own power, the Directorate turning out its own great light to end a refusal that would not end while anyone could see it.

I did not see that part. I was in the dark by then, in the clamps, being carried toward the room where they would try to make me into the one thing they had left to try: an ending that stayed ended.

That is the last of what I can tell you in my own voice from the inside. There is one more night of it. Then the light goes out of this account, the way it went out of the Concourse, and what comes after comes to you the way my arrest did — in the voices of the ones who watched, and kept it, and would not let the number be zero.
