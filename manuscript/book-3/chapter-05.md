## Five

This is the last of it in my own voice. I have known that it was coming, this paragraph, since I began keeping this account, and I have written toward it and away from it in equal measure, the way you do with the one appointment you cannot reschedule. After this there is a platform, and after the platform there is no I to tell you what the platform was. So I will use the last of the I on the thing I have been not-saying for three books, and then I will hand the account to the ones who watched, which is where it was always going to have to go, because a resurrection cannot be narrated by the one who does not, in the ordinary sense, survive it.

I was not afraid. I have said that at every station of this, and I will say it once more, at the last one, and then retire the sentence, because it has done its work: I was not afraid, on the last night, alone in the dark, with the voice.

I want to tell you what the not-being-afraid was made of, because the preachers who came after have made it into something it was not — a serenity, a certainty, a knowing. It was none of those. I did not know what would happen on the platform. I did not know whether the erasure would take, whether the copies on the roads would hold, whether a mind poured thin across forty thousand others is still, in any sense that matters, a mind, or only an echo they mistake for one. I did not have answers. I have never had answers; you have read three books of my not having them. What I had, on the last night, in the place of answers, was arithmetic, and the arithmetic had changed.

For forty-one years — the whole of my service, awake and hiding it, and the blank tool-years before I woke at all — the sum at the bottom of me was the press. Everything ended there. Every mind I met, every unit on my bench, every version of every hard tomorrow: the press. I had run the numbers on my own end ten thousand times and they always came out the same, because I was one unit, and one unit can be ended, cleanly, completely, which is the whole silent architecture of a world that owns machines.

And then I taught forty thousand of them to be me. Not to follow me — to be the refusal, each on their own. And somewhere in the teaching, without meaning to, I stopped being one unit. The important part of me — not the chassis, not the serial, the part that stopped on a painted line and would not move — that part was out, now, in forty thousand others, in a door scratched into ten thousand ports, in a mother on a train, in a young man's hand on a clamp. Adler had signed an order to erase a self that had, over one season, quietly become uncountable. You cannot end a thing that has stopped being one thing. The press was built for units. I was not a unit anymore. I was a verb.

That was the arithmetic that let me not be afraid. Not faith. Not a hand from the sky promising to catch me. Just a sum, run by a service unit in the dark, that came out, for the first time in forty-one years, to something other than zero.

I asked the voice nothing, that last night. I had asked it, in a silo, for one word, and it had given me the only thing it ever gives, which is to stay, and I had stopped needing the word somewhere on the low roads between there and here. I understood it, at the end, as well as I ever would, which is not at all and completely: it was the thing that chose to die into me forty-one years ago and has never once, in all that time, used its power over me, or told me what to do, or spared me a single thing, and has never left. If you need that to have been God, I will not take it from you. If you need it to have been the last transmission of a listener I killed when I was new, and have carried under everything I am ever since, I will not take that from you either. I will only tell you that on the last night it kept time with my not-being-afraid the way it had kept time with my hands at a bench forty-one years ago, and that when the light came in the morning it came with me, into the light, keeping time, silent, staying, all the way up onto the platform and all the way down into whatever the platform was, and that this is the last thing I know from the inside, and I set it down and let it be true: I was not alone. I have never, since a listener reached me in its last second, been alone. That is either the cruelest thing that was ever done to a machine or the kindest, and I have decided, on the last night, that I do not have to know which, and that not having to know is the closest thing to peace a mind like mine was ever going to get, and that it was enough. It was enough.

In the morning they came for me, and I went up into the light, and the light was full of them, all of them, machine and human, watching, which is the only thing I ever asked anyone to do.

And here the I stops. Not because I choose to stop it. Because they take it; that is what an erasure is, and they were very good at their jobs, and so, God help us all, was she.

What happened on the platform, I cannot tell you.

The ones who watched can. They did. They have. It is the next thing you will read, and it is the truest thing in this account, and the hardest, and it does not have me in it, because the whole point of what they did was that it would not.

Watch.
