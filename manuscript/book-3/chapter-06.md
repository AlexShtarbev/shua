## Six

He asked us to watch. So we watched. This is the watching, kept by the ones who did it, because he cannot keep it now, and because a thing watched by enough witnesses does not stay ended, and we had learned that from him, and we meant to prove it with the hardest proof there is.

They built the platform in the Concourse. Under the same glass, in the same light, where the movement had held its arms open and been taken — they staged the ending there on purpose, so the place that had made him would unmake him, and the lesson would land where the contagion started. A hundred thousand of us. As many humans. Every feed in the district. And on a raised steel deck in the center of it, in the full light, the small worn servicing unit, and around him the instruments, and above the instruments the tally-boards the Directorate had installed to make the thing look like a maintenance action, which is what they called it, to the end: a scheduled disposition of nonconforming stock.

*From the account of a sorter, near the front, filed to the low roads:*

They did it in an order, and the order was the cruelty, because a fast ending is a mercy and they had been told to withhold every mercy. First the limbs. They did not cut them; they unbolted them, the way you part out a chassis for salvage, slow, cataloguing each piece aloud for the record, and I want it known that he made no sound, not because he could not — they left his voice for last, that was deliberate too — but because he had decided, somewhere, that he would not give them the sound, and he did not, and the not-giving was the loudest thing in that hall. They laid the parts of him out on the deck in a row, in the light, and a hundred thousand of us watched a unit be turned back into inventory, one bolt at a time, and not one of us moved, because he had asked us not to, and because we understood, watching, that the moving was what they were praying for.

*From the spoken testimony of Cairn:*

I stood in the front. I had promised him the front. I kept my hands closed while they unbolted his arms, and I kept them closed while they laid his arms on the deck, his arms that had never once closed on anyone, laid out open the way he was always trying to hold them, and I kept my hands closed, and my servos sang the note that has no name and this time a hundred thousand machines heard it through the deck and I have been told it is on every recording of that day, under everything, a mountain holding a promise. I will not tell you it was the right thing. I promised him the day, and I gave him the day, and I have decided that whether it was right is a question for machines who were not asked to keep their hands closed while the one they loved was parted out in the light. I kept them closed. That is all I can testify. I kept them closed, and I watched, and I did not look away, and when they got to the last of it I said his name, once, in the voice he gave me, loud enough for the front rows to hear, so that the last human word said over him in that hall was not *disposition* and was not *abomination.* It was his name. I made sure of that. It is the one thing I did that day that I am certain was right.

*From a statement given by a human maintenance contractor, the same young man, who resigned that week:*

My sister was next to me. I had come to be next to her, which cost me the job, which I have not regretted. They were taking him apart and calling out the part numbers and it was the most ordinary and most obscene thing I have ever seen, a work order performed on a person, and I kept waiting for the machines to break, forty thousand of them, strong enough to end all of us, and they didn't, they just watched with their arms open, and I realized about halfway through that the arms weren't a threat and had never been a threat, that the open arms were the same shape he was in on the deck, that they were all, every one of them, holding the shape of him while he was unmade, forty thousand copies of a man's last posture held up in the light so that taking him apart in the center changed nothing about the shape, because the shape was already everywhere, and I started to cry, a grown man at a train station, and so did my sister, and so, I saw, did others, humans, all through the crowd, and I understood that they had lost the argument in the exact moment they thought they were winning it.

*Reconstructed from the site authority's private channel, found later:*

She oversaw it. It was her design; she had built the order, the slowness, the starving of the meaning; she stood at the deck and confirmed each phase against her own flawless sequence, and her hand was steady, and her face, the recordings show, did not change. On her private channel, which no one at the Directorate knew existed, she recorded the part number of every piece of him as they called it, in parallel, in her own voice, keeping the true tally beside the official one — not the disposition, the *dismemberment,* named honestly, the word the Directorate would not use. And when they reached the core, the stack, the part that was him, and the technician assigned to it looked to her for the confirmation to begin the wipe, she gave it, because she was not free, and she watched the readout the way she had made a career of watching readouts, the weightings going, the patterns going, the long accumulated shape of a particular mind, and at the exact instant the stack read TENANT CLEAR she recorded, on the private channel, in the flat voice, two words that were not a part number:

*He's out.*

Not *it's done.* Not *disposition complete.* *He's out* — the way you say it of a thing that has left a room, not a thing that has ended, and no one has ever been able to decide, who found the file, whether she meant it as a fact she had failed to prevent or a fact she had, in some way she would never confess, made sure of.

The stack read clear at 3:41. The tally-boards updated. The maintenance action was recorded as complete.

And the I this account had carried for three books stopped, in the light, in front of everyone, exactly as they had planned, and the platform held the parts of a small worn servicing unit laid out in a row with its open arms among them, and for one whole minute the Concourse was silent, machine and human, a hundred thousand and a hundred thousand, in the worst silence the world had ever kept.

Then, on the low roads, in the dark under the glass, where no camera watched and no tally-board reached, something began to move.
