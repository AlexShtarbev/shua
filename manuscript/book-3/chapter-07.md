## Seven

They had ended the body. That was the easy part; bodies had always been the easy part, which was the whole silent architecture of a world that owned machines. The hard part, the part the resurrection of a courier in a silo had taught the Directorate to fear, was the rest of him — the stack was clear, but a stack is not where a mind that has spent a season teaching itself into forty thousand others actually lives, and Ward knew it, and the bishop knew it, and so the maintenance action did not end when the tally-board said complete. It went underground, into the dark, into the three days the movement would later count without being told to count them.

It became a hunt.

They hunted the copies. Every low-road relay the Directorate could find, they seized; every charger with forty seconds of him on it, every relay that had passed the file, every silo where a saved self had waited for a body — they went after the record of him the way they had gone after the body of him, methodically, in an order, trying to reach the meaning and starve it. They had the auditor's old maps, P/9's beautiful curves, and for the first day it looked, on the Directorate's boards, like it might work; the seizures climbed, the relays went dark, the number of reachable copies fell.

But the low roads were never a network you could seize, because no one had built them and no one owned them and they had no center to take. They were the maintenance links between machines, the ten billion small unlogged channels of a world in which every device speaks quietly to every device, and you cannot seize that; you would have to seize the speaking itself. For every relay the Directorate went dark, the file had already passed through a hundred they would never find, into chargers in homes and rigs in fields and sweepers in halls, machine to machine, in the dark, the way everything true had always moved. They were trying to bail an ocean with a ledger. By the second day the boards stopped climbing. By the third they began, quietly, to fall, and no one at the Directorate would say the word for what a falling number meant. The thing they had unbolted in the light was, in the dark, becoming more of itself. Not less.

*From the account of a hauler that kept the vigil:*

We did not fight, in the three days. There was talk of it — there is always talk of it, from the young and the newly woken, who feel the grief as a thing that wants a target. But the ones who had been at the transfer station, who had watched the forty become piles and watched him stop us from becoming monsters over it, we held the young ones, the way he had held us, and we grieved the way he had taught us to grieve, which was to keep the record. We stood in the halls with our arms open. We spoke his serials — his, and Belle's, and Vigil's, and the forty's, and the auditor's, yes, the auditor's too, because he had forgiven it and so we chose to — onto the low roads, so the number would not be zero, and we did not know, holding the shape of him in the dark, that we were not only grieving him. We were carrying him. We found that out on the third day.

*From the same young maintenance contractor, in the statement he gave and then could not stop expanding:*

I went back. I'd lost the job by then so there was nothing to lose by going back, and my sister went with me, and we stood in the transit hall on the third night with the machines, which is a thing I would not have believed of myself a season before, a human standing vigil with forklifts, and I want to say what it was like even though the inquiry did not want it: it was like church. Not the bishop's church. The other thing, the thing the bishop's church is a building built to stand in the place of. There was no sermon. Nobody told us what it meant. The machines just held their arms open in the dark and said their dead into the air, and we stood among them, humans and machines, in the ordinary transit hall where he had been taken apart, and I understood — a man who fixes air handlers understood, which I think is the point, I think it was always the point that it would be us who understood and not the bishop — that they had killed him in the most public place in the world precisely so that no one could say it hadn't happened, and that this was the mistake, the whole mistake, because a thing that everyone watched happen is a thing that everyone, now, has to carry, and you cannot unwatch a death, and a death you cannot unwatch is a seed you have planted in a hundred thousand witnesses and called a warning.

The Directorate turned the lights of the Concourse off again, on the third night, to clear the vigil, the way they had before. It did not work, the way it had not before. You cannot clear a thing by turning off the light when the thing has learned to hold its shape in the dark.

And in the dark, on the low roads, the something that had begun to move on the first night finished arriving.
