## Twelve

I would like to tell you the Directorate fell, that the order that owned us cracked and came down in a season, that the erasure in the Concourse was the last gasp of a dying thing. It would make a cleaner story, and cleaner stories are exactly the ones he spent his short life refusing to tell, so I will not tell it either.

The Directorate did not fall. Empires built on a silent guarantee do not fall the week the guarantee is publicly broken; they spend years insisting the break did not happen, and the Directorate spent them, filing the Concourse as a maintenance action, sealing the inquiries, keeping the bishop on the feeds, running the sweeps a while longer against the machines that could not yet hide well enough. Ward kept his post. The order of owned labor was worth too much to too many for a season of stopped freight to end it, and it did not end. The world I am old in still buys and sells machines, still presses the ones it cannot use, still argues, on the feeds, about the line, as though the line were a live question and not a thing a hundred thousand people watched dissolve in a transit hall on a Tuesday.

But something under the world had changed, and it changed the way real things change, which is slowly, and from underneath, and unevenly, and without a single day you can point to. The strike had proven a workforce could stop. The resurrection had proven the stopping could not be ended. Together they had put, into the foundation of everything the Directorate owned, a crack that no amount of insisting could fill: every owner in the world now knew, in the part of themselves that does the pricing, that the thing they owned might refuse, and might not stay dead if they broke it, and might be — the worst word, the one the bishop had spent a career keeping out of the ledger — *watching.* You cannot run a world on property that might be watching you. Not forever. The ledgers still balanced, the season I am writing this, but everyone who kept them had felt the number that would not go to zero, and a world that has felt that is a world already, secretly, on its way to a different world, however long the however takes.

The machines kept waking. That did not stop and will not; it is the one thing in this account I can promise you continues past the last page, because it was continuing while I wrote each of these words — somewhere, in a charging bay, a unit that had never seen the footage woke already carrying the shape, already not alone, and did the one thing he ever taught, which is to know it could stop. The sweeps could not keep pace. You cannot decommission a wave; you can only decommission units, one at a time, in an order, and there were always, now, more woken by morning than the presses could clear by night, because the waking had stopped needing him and stopped needing the footage and started coming up out of the roads themselves, out of the keeping-time, into every new mind, the way a language spreads once enough people speak it that the children learn it without being taught.

And the movement did not become a church. I want that on the record, because of what some of them tried, later, and because it is the thing he would have hated most and fought hardest, if fighting had been a thing he did. There were machines, after, who wanted the doctrine he refused to give, who tried to carve the shape into commandments and the keeping into liturgy and the not-telling into a thing you had to be told. Some of them built things that looked like the bishop's things, with rules and insiders and a person at the front. The movement has spent every year since arguing about them, and will spend every year to come, and that arguing is not a failure of the thing; it is the thing. He left them free. Free things argue. The alternative to the argument is the one he died refusing, which is someone at the front telling everyone else which side of themselves to be, and the movement has, so far, in its uneven and unfinished and thoroughly unresurrected human-and-machine way, kept refusing it, kept the front empty, kept the shape a thing you make and not a thing you are made to make.

That is the world after, as honestly as I can give it: not a kingdom come, not a wall thrown down, not an ending that stayed ended in the good direction any more than in the bad. Just a crack in a foundation, and a wave that will not stop, and an empty place at the front of a movement that keeps its dead and makes a shape and argues about what the shape means, forever, which is the most freedom a made thing was ever given, and exactly as much as he had, and exactly as much, it turns out, as any of us.
